Selected work

Three threads run through my research — physical AI, thermal-fluid modeling, and inverse optics sensing — held together by high-performance computing. Most projects sit in more than one.

Differentiable physics & inverse problems

MIT · 2025–present

Differentiable rendering for hyperspectral flame tomography

A differentiable-rendering framework for gradient-based 3-D reconstruction of temperature and species fields from hyperspectral images, built on GPU-accelerated non-gray radiative transfer in CUDA, JAX, and Julia, with diffusion and attention priors for sparse-view reconstruction.

MIT · 2023–present

PDE-constrained inverse modeling of energetic materials

PDE-constrained sensitivity methods that infer material properties and chemical kinetics from thermal imaging, thermocouple, and spectrometer measurements of propagating reaction fronts. Differentiable solvers with adjoint sensitivities make the estimation scalable, and Bayesian inference over reaction-diffusion models keeps results physically consistent and uncertainty-aware.

Machine learning for physical systems

MIT · 2025–present

Digital twins from sparse sensor data

A global–local attention framework that fuses a learned global latent state with local sensor observations to reconstruct and forecast physical fields — including components no sensor observes — with quantified uncertainty with generative flow matching, trained on reacting-flow LES generated in OpenFOAM with detailed chemistry.

MIT · 2026–present

Reinforcement learning design of experiments

Reinforcement learning combined with greedy active learning to decide what measurement to take next, generating custom wavelength-modulation spectroscopy waveforms at roughly 1 ms latency and accelerating information gain 20×–100× over state-of-the-art methods.

High-performance & distributed simulation

Oak Ridge National Laboratory / UConn · 2020–2024

CUDA Monte Carlo ray tracing for emitting media

Led development of a CUDA 3-D forward ray-tracing solver with a line-by-line spectral database, coupled to OpenFOAM. BVH-based parallelization gave a >400× speedup and scaled to 1,500 GPUs on a top-3 supercomputer; later work with Kokkos and MPI addressed the communication bottlenecks.

Google Research · 2024

Lagrangian particle tracking on TPUs

A scalable multi-TPU Lagrangian particle-tracking solver for differentiable wildfire CFD in Swirl-LM, tested to 250 TPU cores. Optimizing SPMD execution meant reading TensorFlow and XLA HLO IR directly — which also surfaced a compiler bug I reported.

About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Mechanical Engineering and Computational Science at MIT, finishing in 2027. My research sits between three fields that increasingly need each other: physical AI, thermal-fluid modeling, and inverse optics sensing.

In practice that means building the solver and the inference method together — CUDA and JAX radiative transfer, adjoint-based PDE-constrained estimation, attention and diffusion models for sparse reconstruction — and then making the whole thing run at the scale the problem actually demands, whether that is 1,500 GPUs on a leadership-class machine or 250 TPU cores at Google Research. Much of it is anchored by real measurements: hyperspectral images, laser spectra, thermocouples and thermal cameras.

The applications have been combustion, fire, propulsion, and materials processing, but the methods are general: recovering what you cannot measure directly from what you can.
